# Juniper
*AI copilot for couples navigating major financial life transitions.*

*Last updated: 2026-08-28 by kuba-vault*

---

## TL;DR

Juniper helps engaged and newly married couples model the financial tradeoffs behind big decisions (buying a home, combining finances, paying down debt, planning for a baby, prenups) and align on them before commitments are made. All five domains run end-to-end in production at `www.juniperplan.com` (apex 308s to `www`, and the `juniper-api-server.vercel.app` alias 307s to the canonical host); the phase is post-MVP GTM iteration. **Plaid Production is now proven, not just approved:** five real institutions are linked live, reading about $97,692 of net worth (Carter Bank & Trust, Chase credit card, PayPal, Charles Schwab brokerage plus a Roth Contributory IRA, and Marcus by Goldman Sachs). **The fourteen PRs merged on 2026-08-26 (#129 to #142) are what made those numbers worth reading:** before them every app surface fell back to a seeded demo household, so a signed-in member saw $67,330 of somebody else's net worth, a fabricated 726 credit score, invented subscriptions, demo plans, and a spending donut that counted their own credit-card payment as money spent. That fallback is deleted rather than gated: `FinanceSource` is now `manual` or `live`, spending counts consumption only, Plans and Overview and the Score all read the member's own plans through one hook, Credit shows only the cards their bank reports, and the two surfaces that could not clear the bar (Recommended and the shared partner workspace) are unrouted. The six PRs after that made the money surfaces honest: the Juniper Score now divides by the history the member actually has and drops credit health entirely when it has no credit data (#146), each score factor is drawn against a target derived from the member's own income and spending (#148), net worth is reconstructed backward from transactions to before the join date and marked as an estimate (#147), real bank marks reach the Overview rollup and the Credit cards (#143), "Add account" moved into the Connections header (#144), and an accepted invite lands on the plan instead of a not-found card (#145). Two things gate the next step: migration `0015_networth_estimated` is **not applied yet**, so the backfill's write still fails on an unknown column, and the Stage 6 compliance pair (financial-data TOS and privacy policy, plus a security review) is still open before broad public linking. Affiliate URLs are still `example.com`.

## What it is

**The problem:** Couples make huge financial decisions (home, debt, kids, prenups) without forward-looking modeling, and money conversations between partners are emotionally loaded and often avoided.
**The solution:** A neutral AI copilot that runs each partner through the same scripted dialogue, synthesizes a shared plan, and shows side-by-side where they agree and where they don't.
**The user:** Engaged or newly married couples, late 20s to early 40s.
**The value:** A structured, low-emotion way to surface tradeoffs and align before the irrevocable decision gets made.

## Where we are right now

**Real banks are linked in production. The path is proven, not just approved.** Five live institutions are connected on the production account with real balances: Carter Bank & Trust, Chase (credit card), PayPal, Charles Schwab (brokerage plus a Roth Contributory IRA), and Marcus by Goldman Sachs. Net worth reads about $97,692. That closes the open loop that stood since 2026-08-25, and it retires the "approved but unproven" caveat that used to sit in this section and in Risks.

**Six PRs landed on top of that, and five of them were about telling the member the truth.** #146 fixed two bugs that inflated the Juniper Score for anyone newly linked: `api/_finance-snapshot.ts` divided income and spending by a constant three months regardless of how much history existed, and `creditFactor` returned a flat 70 with no credit data. On a real member the score went from 92 "Excellent" with the lever "improve your credit health" to 79 "Healthy" with the lever "build your emergency fund" (82 once a card reported its limit), and the emergency-fund factor dropped from 87 to 29, which is the truth: 1.7 months of cover, not 5.2. #148 then replaced the five identical 0-100 bars on the Score page with one rail per factor, each showing the member's own amount against a target derived from their own income and spending. #147 rebuilt the net-worth trend backward from transactions so it reaches before the join date, and marks those points as estimates. #143 and #144 pushed real institution logos onto the Overview rollup, the Credit cards, and the picker's search rows, and moved "Add account" out of a card roughly 1,400px down the Connections page into the page header. #145 fixed accepted partner invites, which had been landing on a not-found card.

**The week before that is what made those numbers safe to show.** On 2026-08-26 fourteen PRs (#129 to #142) took the seeded demo household out of the product, one surface at a time. Balances stopped waiting on a transaction feed that arrives minutes later or never (#132). Spending stopped counting transfers, Venmo, and credit-card payments as money spent, which had been billing the member twice for the same coffee (#136). Credit dropped the invented 726 score and started showing the real cards and their utilization (#131, #138). Plans, Overview, and the Score page moved onto one shared `useMemberPlans()` hook, so the plan a member created at signup finally appears and the Score's "ways to improve" stop pointing at a demo plan (#134, #135, #141). Overview lost its fabricated Subscriptions panel and the client seam lost `MOCK` entirely (#140). The institution gallery was dropped for Plaid's own list (#129, #130, #139), and the two surfaces that could not be made to show the member's own data, Recommended and the shared partner workspace, were unrouted rather than faked (#142). The rule in `CLAUDE.md` (only the member's own real data may render) dates from that week.

**The one blocking ops step is migration `0015_networth_estimated`.** It is written and committed but not applied to the production Supabase DB, so `api/plaid/networth-backfill.ts` fails its write on the unknown `estimated` column and the net-worth trend stays exactly as it is. Same shape as the `0008` and `0014` "ops to activate" steps tracked in ROADMAP.md.

**The second thing to know about the backfill is a Plaid consent rule, not a bug.** Plaid's Data Transparency Messaging only allows a product to be added to an existing item if it was named in `additional_consented_products` at link time. `api/plaid/link-token.ts` only started consenting to `investments` in #144, so the member's existing Schwab item cannot serve `/investments/transactions/get` and carries its invested balance back flat. Schwab is about 73% of their net worth, so that is a real limitation on the reconstructed trend rather than a footnote. New links are fine; the fix for the existing one is a relink.

**Still open, and not re-litigated:** confirm `PLAID_REDIRECT_URI` in Vercel names the canonical origin (`https://www.juniperplan.com/app/connections`), since it is one All-Environments value and an OAuth bank that returns to a different origin lands with no Supabase session; the Stage 6 compliance pair (financial-data TOS + privacy policy, security review of the data surfaces) before broad public linking; and affiliate URLs are still `example.com` placeholders pending licensing.

**Fast health probe** without signing in: `curl -X POST https://juniper-api-server.vercel.app/api/plaid/link-token -d '{}'` returns **401** when credentials are present and **503** when `plaidConfigured()` is false, which is the quickest way to confirm Vercel picked up an env change. Plaid's real failure reason is logged server-side in `api/plaid/link-token.ts` (`[plaid] link/token/create failed`), readable through the Vercel MCP `get_runtime_logs` scoped to `environment: production` with a short `since` window (a wide window times out).

**Recorded elsewhere, not repeated here:** the Plaid Production resolution of 2026-08-25 (wrong-account root cause, the Vercel env split, the registered redirect URIs, the Supabase auth URL config) now lives in the Decisions log and Notable debug lessons, since it is settled history rather than current state.

**Backend / data**
- Vercel Edge Functions in `api/`: `chat.ts`, `dialogue.ts`, `plans.ts`, `plan-chat.ts`, `invites.ts`, `profile.ts`, `waitlist.ts`, plus `plaid/{link-token,exchange,accounts,remove,institutions-search,institution-logos,transactions-sync,networth-snapshot,networth-backfill,layer-session}.ts`
- **Plaid edge endpoints** call Plaid directly over `fetch` (no SDK, edge-safe): `link-token` (creates a Link token), `exchange` (public token → access token, stored server-side), `accounts` (returns sanitized snapshots), `remove` (unlinks). Helpers `api/_plaid.ts` (Plaid fetch wrapper) and `api/_supabase-admin.ts` (service-role client). The Plaid `access_token` never leaves the server
- **Spending means consumption, and every category means something** *(PR #136, `api/_categorize.ts`)*: `/api/finances` used to read only the sign of a transaction amount, so every outflow was spending and every inflow was income. A transfer to savings, a Venmo to a friend, and a credit-card payment all counted as money spent, and the card payment was the worst of it, because the purchases behind it already counted when they happened, so the member was billed twice in the same month. `_categorize.ts` is now the single taxonomy table: **11 groups** (9 spend, 1 income, 1 transfer) over **46 leaf categories**, driven by Plaid's *detailed* `personal_finance_category` with the primary as a floor. Each group carries a `kind`, and three rules follow from it: `transfer` is ignored entirely, `income` is netted from its own categories, and `spend` is summed **signed**, so a refund reduces the category it came back to instead of masquerading as income. Groups are derived at read time and never stored, and `spent` is *defined as* the sum of the breakdown, so the card header, the donut center, and the cashflow strip cannot disagree. The same three rules run in `api/_finance-snapshot.ts`, which is why this reaches past the dashboard: those two numbers feed the Juniper Score's savings rate and emergency-fund factor. On the worked example in the PR, a July went from $5,844 spent / $6,535 income to $3,384 / $6,400
- **Balances ship without waiting on transactions, and one dead item no longer blocks a refresh** *(PR #132)*: `api/finances.ts` gates per section instead of behind one AND-gate. `netWorth` and `accounts` ship the moment any account source exists (Plaid snapshot or `manual_accounts`); `cashflow`, `spending`, `budgets`, and `transactions` ship only once transaction rows exist, and absent sections are omitted rather than zero-filled, because a zero would overwrite a real figure the member gave us. `{ linked: false }` now means all three sources are empty, and a `hasTransactions` boolean rides along so the client gates on a real signal. Both sync endpoints isolate per item: a refused item is logged with its Plaid error code, recorded in `failures[]` (flagged `needs_relink` for `ITEM_LOGIN_REQUIRED` and `INVALID_ACCESS_TOKEN`), and skipped, with a non-200 only when every item fails; `transactions-sync` deliberately does not persist a cursor for a failed item, since replay is safe. A partial run refreshes per-item balances but reports `snapshot_skipped` and writes no trend point. Client side, `syncFinancesUntilTransactions()` retries only the transactions leg with bounded exponential backoff, so a link whose first pull is not ready yet still lands
- **`api/plaid/institutions-search.ts` and `api/plaid/institution-logos.ts`** *(PRs #129, #133, #144)*: search POSTs Plaid `/institutions/search` filtered by `PLAID_PRODUCTS`, so nothing offered can fail at link time, and returns `institution_id`, `name`, `oauth`, `routing_number`, plus `logo` and `primary_color` from `include_optional_metadata`. The logo endpoint takes institution ids **only** from the caller's own `plaid_items` rows via the service-role client, deduped, oldest first, capped at 25, so it cannot be used as an authenticated Plaid enumeration proxy; a Plaid refusal is logged and skipped rather than failing the request, and the response carries `Cache-Control: private, max-age=604800`
- **The Juniper Score is computed off the history the member actually has** *(PR #146, `api/_score.ts` + `api/_finance-snapshot.ts`, mirrored in `src/lib/score.ts`)*. `_finance-snapshot.ts` used to divide income and spending by a constant three months (`WINDOW_DAYS / 30`) regardless of how much history existed; it now divides by the days the transactions actually span, floored at 14 (`MIN_COVERED_DAYS`) and capped at the 90-day window (`coveredDays`). `creditFactor` no longer returns a flat 70 when there is no credit data: it returns `null`, `computeScore` filters it out, and the remaining weights renormalize to sum to 1, so each factor's published "% of score" is what it actually is. Where the data does exist it is used: revolving utilization is computed from linked cards that report a `limit`, counting only those cards so a limitless card cannot inflate the ratio, and before manual balances fold in
- **Net-worth backfill** *(PR #147)*: `api/plaid/networth-backfill.ts` walks backward from today's balances through the transactions that moved them (`cash(day-1) = cash(day) + that day's depository total`, `owed(day-1) = owed(day) - that day's credit total`, `invest(day-1) = invest(day) - external inflow`). The pure arithmetic lives in `api/_networth-walk.ts`, split out following the `_score.ts` convention, because the signs are the whole correctness story. Investments cannot be reconstructed exactly (market movement is not a transaction and Plaid has no historical price feed), so the invested balance is today's holdings minus net contributions since, read from `/investments/transactions/get`. Flows are classified by **subtype, not type**, because Plaid files `contribution` under `type: buy` and `distribution` under `type: sell`; direction also comes from the subtype, because Plaid signs a combined contribution-and-purchase positive like a buy. `BACKFILL_MAX_DAYS` is 365. Rows are written with `Prefer: resolution=ignore-duplicates`, so a day Juniper actually recorded always beats a reconstruction
- `api/plaid/link-token.ts` sends `additional_consented_products: ["investments"]` (PR #144), so items linked from that point on can serve `/investments/transactions/get`. Items linked before it cannot, and need a relink
- `api/_dialogue-scripts.ts` mirrors the frontend script indices; structured (tap-first) steps are stubbed server-side since they no longer need an LLM turn. Partner-mode aware (skips inviter-only steps)

## Decisions log

- **2026-08-28, score-factor targets are computed in the engine, not on the page** - `FactorGauge` hangs off `Factor` in `api/_score.ts` (mirrored in `src/lib/score.ts`), so each target sits next to the formula it comes from: 20% of income beside the savings-rate calculation, six months of spending beside the emergency-fund calculation, 0.3x annual income beside the debt calculation, one year of income beside the investing calculation, 30% beside the utilization calculation. A page that recomputed them would be a second definition of the same thresholds, free to drift from the scoring. `gauge` is `null` where a target cannot honestly be stated: with no income there is no savings target, and for a bureau credit score there is no target at all, because naming a "good score" threshold is exactly the kind of invention #146 removed.
- **2026-08-28, reconstructed net-worth points are marked `estimated` and drawn dashed, not presented as observations** - the backfill is honest arithmetic on real transactions, but the invested portion carries no market movement (Plaid has no historical price feed, and `/investments/transactions` returns flows, not prices). Migration `0015_networth_estimated` adds the flag, `/api/finances` returns it per point, and `NetWorthChart` splits the line so the member can see which part of their own history was reconstructed. Rejected: presenting both kinds of point identically, which would have made a reconstruction look like a measurement. Backfilled rows are written with `resolution=ignore-duplicates`, so a day Juniper actually recorded always wins over a reconstruction of the same day.
- **2026-08-27, the credit-score question is answered: VantageScore 3.0, and it is not a licensing problem** - per `docs/CREDIT_PROVIDER.md` (PR #137). FICO Score Open Access does not apply to Juniper twice over: it is a fee waiver for lenders already buying scores, and its counselling track requires a 501(c)(3) or a government agency. VantageScore 3.0 is the route: consumer disclosure is an enumerated permitted use in the base agreement, the trademark licence is royalty-free, and bureau pricing fell to roughly $1 per pull in late 2025. Free to the end user is therefore a margin decision, not a licensing one, and **compliance dominates the cost**, not the score. Treat this as settled: do not re-research it.
- **2026-08-26, the Score page keeps one table of what counts as a plan for each factor** - `FACTOR_ROUTES` in `pages/app/score.tsx` gives every factor a match half (which plan shape, plus a word list) and an offer half (which template to create, or Ask Juniper). The word lists are deliberately narrower than the app-wide `SHAPE_KEYWORDS`, because "fund" alone would let a baby fund answer for an emergency fund. Savings rate matches nothing on purpose: a rate has nothing to save toward, nothing to buy, and no balance to clear, so all three shapes are fictions for it. ## Notable debug lessons

Subtle enough to repeat, so worth keeping a section:

- **A fixed averaging window over a variable history inflates every ratio built on it.** `_finance-snapshot.ts` divided by a constant 90 days while a member had three weeks of transactions. That did not make the numbers noisy, it made them wrong in a consistent and flattering direction: emergency fund read 3x the months it actually covers, investing pace 3x the pace, debt load a third of the burden. The only figure that survived was savings rate, and only by luck, because it is a ratio of two numbers that were both a third too small. Any "per month" figure must divide by the history that exists, not the window that was requested.
- **A neutral placeholder inside a weighted score is not neutral.** `creditFactor` returned a flat 70 when there was no credit data. That was 10.5 points of a score presented to the member as their own, and because a 70 leaves the most headroom, it won the "biggest lever" ranking, so the app's single loudest instruction was to improve the one thing it had no data about. The fix is to drop an unmeasured component and renormalize the remaining weights, never to fill it in with a plausible number.
- **Reading the sign of a transaction is not reading what happened to the money.** `/api/finances` treated every outflow as spending and every inflow as income, so a transfer to savings, a Venmo to a friend, and a credit-card payment all counted as money spent. The card payment was the worst case and the one nobody spots by eyeballing a total: the purchases it pays for already counted when they happened, so the member was billed twice in the same month for the same coffee. Only the category can tell a card payment from a car payment, and Plaid's *detailed* `personal_finance_category` is the level that carries it (the primary cannot). The blast radius reached past the dashboard, because spending and income feed the Score's savings rate and emergency-fund factor. Anything that classifies money by sign alone is wrong in the same way.
- **One AND-gate over two independent sources withholds both, and a loop that returns on first failure turns one bad item into an outage.** `api/finances.ts` required items AND transactions before returning anything, so real, already-stored balances were queued behind a feed that arrives minutes later and sometimes never. In the same PR: `networth-snapshot.ts` returned 502 the instant one item's balance call failed, and the snapshot rewrite sat inside that loop, so one broken connection meant nothing refreshed at all, while the caller discarded the response body and showed no error. Gate per section, isolate per item, and report what failed.
- **Deleting demo data makes degenerate shapes reachable for the first time.** Removing the mock layer meant `NetWorthChart` could finally receive a one-point series, a dead-flat series, and a negative series; it divided by `series.length - 1` and scaled bounds by 0.985/1.01, so all three produced NaN paths or inverted bounds. The same removal exposed a delta chip hardcoded to "up", safe only while the number came from a demo household that always rose. Seed data is not just wrong content, it is a shape filter hiding the empty and edge cases.
- **Check the account before the config** - a Plaid entitlement that looks inexplicably stuck is more likely the wrong account than a broken setting. Five months of "Production under review" were a different client ID's dashboard. Same shape applies to any per-account SaaS console.
- **Probe env changes without signing in** - `curl -X POST .../api/plaid/link-token -d '{}'` returns 401 when creds are present and 503 when `plaidConfigured()` is false, which distinguishes "Vercel has not picked up the env yet" from "auth is rejecting me" in one call. Plaid's real reason lands in the server log (`[plaid] link/token/create failed`), and the Vercel runtime-log query needs a short `since` window or it times out.
- **Env vars with embedded newlines** — `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` had a `\n` at position 208 from a copy-paste. Node fetch headers reject CR/LF. `api/_env.ts` strips non-printable chars from all server-side env reads.
- **ES256 vs HS256** — Newer Supabase projects use asymmetric JWT signing keys (ES256). Legacy "JWT Secret" doesn't sign user tokens anymore. `api/_supabase-jwt.ts` checks `header.alg` and routes to JWKS (ES256) or HMAC (HS256) accordingly.
- **GRANT for raw-SQL tables** — Tables created via SQL migration don't auto-grant Data API access. Every new table needs `GRANT SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE ON public.<table> TO authenticated;` or every request returns silent 401.
- **Unescaped backticks in template literals** — `BASE` prompt had backticked angle brackets inside a backtick-delimited string. JS parser closed the template early and tried to interpret the rest as tagged template expressions ("X is not a function" at module load). Don't use backticks in prose inside backtick-delimited strings.
- **PostgREST OR filter quirks** — `or=(user_id.eq.X,partner_user_id.eq.X)` returned `[]` even when matching rows existed. Switched to two parallel queries + client-side merge.
- **Double-read of fetch response body** — `/api/plans` GET error path was reading `.text()` after `.json()`; fixed by buffering once and reusing.

- `PLAID_ENV` (backend) - `production` scoped to Production only, `sandbox` scoped to All Pre-Production; local `.env` stays `sandbox`
- `PLAID_PRODUCTS` (backend, optional, default `transactions` since PR #122; was `auth`, which Juniper never needed), `PLAID_COUNTRY_CODES` (backend, optional, default `US`), `PLAID_REDIRECT_URI` (backend, All Environments) - only for OAuth banks, and the value must be registered in the Plaid dashboard of the *same* account as `PLAID_CLIENT_ID`. Registered on the live account: `https://juniperplan.com/app/connections`, `https://www.juniperplan.com/app/connections`, `https://juniper-api-server.vercel.app/app/connections`
- `PLAID_LAYER_TEMPLATE_ID` (backend, optional) — Plaid Layer template id; when unset, `/api/plaid/layer-session` returns 503 and the client falls back to the gallery. Set it (with Production) to enable tier-1 phone-first discovery
- `VITE_PLAID_LAYER` (frontend, optional) — mounts the `LayerDiscovery` phone-entry step in onboarding + Connections. `1`/`live` = real Plaid Layer (needs Production + `PLAID_LAYER_TEMPLATE_ID`); `demo` = simulated discovery testable on Sandbox (recognized accounts are mocked and imported as manual accounts); unset = off
- `VITE_SIGNUP_INVITE_CODE` (frontend, optional) — gates open signups during private preview

Plaid + service-role vars are set in Vercel on Production and Preview, with `PLAID_ENV`/`PLAID_SECRET` scoped so only Production is Plaid Production. Supabase auth URL config (separate from Plaid's allow-list, and not an env var): Site URL `https://www.juniperplan.com/`, Redirect URLs `https://juniperplan.com/**`, `https://www.juniperplan.com/**`, `https://juniper-api-server.vercel.app/**`. The app builds `emailRedirectTo` from `window.location.origin` in `pages/auth/sign-up.tsx`, so every origin it is served from must be in that allow-list or Supabase silently falls back to the Site URL.
