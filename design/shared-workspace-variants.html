D is the hybrid you asked for and sits at the bottom. A, B and C above are the originals, kept for comparison. Three readings of "switching space should change the whole product, and start near blank." Every one of them assumes the sharing defect is fixed first: nothing crosses to the other person until it is explicitly shared. What differs is where the shared thing lives and how you put it there.

A. Shared is the same product, in a different context

Same six tabs, every one of them scoped to the couple. Overview, Transactions, Plans, Ask Juniper, Credit and Connections all keep their meaning and answer "for us" instead of "for me". Switching genuinely changes the whole app.

Biggest build: six surfaces need a shared branch, and Credit for two people is a design question nobody has answered

B. Shared is its own smaller product, with its own nav

Switching swaps the nav for the six shared surfaces that were already written: Overview, Accounts, Goals, Bills, Activity, Sharing. It stops pretending every personal feature has a couple version, and four of these pages exist already.

Cheapest by far, but the nav changing shape under you is a jolt, and "my whole product changes" becomes "a different, smaller product"

C. Shared is a canvas the two of you build

One surface that opens genuinely blank and grows only what you put on it. You add an account, a plan, a bill or a goal from either side, and the nav grows a tab the moment the first thing of that kind lands. Closest to the blank canvas you described.

Most new design, and the nav appearing tab by tab is a pattern nobody else in the app uses
