A credit card entered by hand, on the Credit page (migration 0046)
The states the Credit page can now draw, against the real stylesheet. Not a set of variants: the treatment was decided in the plan, and this exists so the copy and the badges can be read rather than described. The figures are the reconciliation case: a $20,000 Chase Freedom Unlimited that is an authorized-user card on another person's login, which Plaid can never return, against three cards Juniper does see.
1 Utilization, before and after
The whole point of the feature. Before: three linked cards, $17,900 of limit, and the figure runs high because the denominator is short by a card Juniper cannot see. After: the hand-entered card joins the sum and the page agrees with the member's own credit report. The sentence under the figure is the existing #211 disclosure, which now covers both kinds of member-supplied limit.

2 The three provenance states, side by side
The badges are the load-bearing part. No badge means the bank reported the limit: a fact. You set this (#211) means the member typed a limit for a card the bank DOES report, where only the limit was missing. You added this (0046) means the member is the source of the card's existence as well as its limit. Three different claims, so they do not share a label.

3 A hand-entered card with no limit yet
It reads "no limit added", not "limit not reported": there is no bank reporting anything, and blaming the absence on a report the member never expected reads as a fault rather than as a field they have not filled in. The link is the way out of the dead end, and it says Edit, which it can now that the Connections row has an in-place editor. It read "Manage" when this shipped, because Connections offered add and remove only and promising an editor would have sent the member looking for a control that was not there.

4 The form, with and without the credit-only fields
Limit and mask appear only on Credit cards. Not tidiness: 0046's CHECK makes a limit on a checking account unrepresentable and the endpoint rejects it with a 400, so a field that could collect one would be a field that could only produce an error. The note says what the limit is for and, more importantly, that it does not move the Juniper Score.
The limit lets Juniper work out your utilization across every card, including ones your bank cannot show it. It is your number, so it is labelled as yours wherever it appears, and it does not affect your Juniper Score.
