Setting a credit limit your bank does not report, three treatments (issue #211)
Real state from production: Chase reports a $9,000 limit, Capital One ····5012 ($328 owed) and Discover ····2776 ($0 owed) report none, so overall utilization is built from one card and says "2 more excluded for reporting no limit". All three treatments let the member supply the two missing numbers. What differs is where the control lives.
Two rules hold in all three, and the second one is the reason this is not just a text field.
1. A limit you typed is never drawn as a limit your bank reported. It carries a "You set this" badge everywhere it appears, and the utilization line says how many of its limits came from you. The two are different kinds of number: one is a fact, the other is a claim, and a percentage built from a mix of them is only as good as the claim.
2. It does not touch your Juniper Score. The Score's credit factor is computed from bank-reported limits only, deliberately, so nobody can move their own score by typing a generous number. If every limit on a card is self-reported, that factor stays unmeasured and the Score keeps saying so, which is the behaviour #146 built.
A Inline on the row that is missing it
The control sits exactly where the gap is stated. "limit not reported" gains a "Set limit" link, and the row expands into a field in place. Cheapest to build, nothing new above the fold, and the member is looking at the card's name and mask while they type, which is what stops them entering the Chase limit against the Capital One row. The trade: with three unlimited cards there are three separate links and no single place that says "you have two of these to do".

C Where the consequence is already stated
The exclusion line becomes the control. "2 more excluded for reporting no limit" is already the sentence that tells the member something is missing and already the only place the cost of it is visible, so it gains a link that opens both cards in one small panel under the hero. No new banner, no per-row links, and the fix sits next to the number it fixes. The trade: it is the least discoverable of the three, because nobody reads the small print under a big percentage until they are already wondering why it looks wrong.
