Card faces and the wallet
Your four real cards. One face design proposed below, then three ways of showing them together. The face is a single proposal rather than a third axis, because face style times wallet behaviour would be nine combinations and that is not a useful choice.
Still no issuer card art, and that has not changed. Those renders are licensed brand assets and Juniper has no relationship with any card issuer, which is the same gate holding the marketplace. What is below is drawn entirely from the product's stored brand colour plus the issuer's own logo as served by Plaid, which the app is already licensed for and already draws on Connections.
The network is set in plain type, never as its own logo. "VISA" as a word states a fact about the card you hold. Reproducing Visa's mark and colours would be using their brand asset, which is exactly the line this component exists to stay on the right side of.
The face, one proposal
Same inputs as today, more care in the drawing. Two gradient stops derived in CSS from the one stored colour, so nothing new is stored per product and a flat hex reads as a moulded surface. A real EMV chip with contact lines, a generic contactless glyph, the product name letter-spaced and shadowed to read as embossing, the network in plain type, and the mask where a mask belongs. The unidentified state stays an outline, because there is no brand to borrow yet and borrowing one would make an unanswered card look answered.

A finding: the face needs a shorter name than the catalog stores
The catalog deliberately stores the name as the issuer spells it, because that is what makes a member recognizing their own card a straight string comparison, and it is what the picker has to show. It is also 53 characters for "Capital One Quicksilver Student Cash Rewards Credit Card", which truncates on a 196px face and even on the 236px one in treatment C.

Derived, not stored. Eighteen hand-written short names would be eighteen chances to get one wrong and would need a migration every time the catalog grows; one function with cases is checkable. It drops the issuer (already printed above it) and the generic tails, and it keeps the issuer when removing it would leave the name starting on a lowercase word, which is the case that broke the first version: "Discover it® Chrome" became "it® Chrome".

A Wallet pocket, cards stacked vertically
Closest to the thing you liked. The cards sit in a pocket with a lip over the front one, each revealing its own top strip, and tapping one brings it forward. Vertical reveal is the reason to prefer it: a 54px strip carries the issuer, the network and the product name, so a fifth and sixth card cost 54px each rather than running out of horizontal room. Collapse gives the page its space back.

Two costs, both real. It is the tallest of the three at 290px against the 132px the shipped fan uses, so the hero's stats sit beside it on a wide screen and under it on a narrow one. And it forces the product name to the top of the card, off the bottom where embossing actually lives: the first draft of this treatment used a 34px strip with the name at the bottom, and both Chase cards revealed only "CHASE" and were impossible to tell apart.

B Horizontal fan that spreads on hover
The smallest change, and it keeps the hero exactly where it is. The fan already ships; this gives it the better faces, a 52px reveal, and a lift on hover or tap that brings one card clear of the others. Nothing else on the Credit page moves and it stays at 352 by 132px.

Three things to know, all of them found by rendering it rather than by reasoning about it. The front card is the one on the right, which is not a style choice: with the leftmost card on top every hidden card shows its right edge, and that is where the network name sits, so the fan read "VISA VISA RCARD COVER" until the stacking was reversed. Hover lifts one card rather than spreading the fan, because a full spread of four cards needs 816px, the entire hero width, and measurably put the rightmost card at 846px inside a 352px box, straight over the stats. And the 52px reveal shows enough to tell two cards apart but not to read a name: you get the issuer, the first word or so, and the mask, which is what the lift is for.

The hard ceiling is the reason to hesitate. Four cards is 352px, five is 404px, a sixth stops fitting beside the stats. Right for four, wrong if this member links two more, which the missing Freedom Unlimited makes likely.

C One card at a time, with that card's own numbers
The only one where a card's own figures are the subject. A full-size face with its balance, limit, utilization and best categories beside it, and arrows or dots to move between cards. Reads best on a phone, and it is the only treatment that answers "what is THIS card doing" without a second click. The trade is the thing you said you liked: you cannot see all your cards together, and the aggregate stats have to live somewhere else on the page.
