The member arranges their own Overview, three treatments (issue #251)
Eight widgets, the seven the Overview draws today plus Cards and rewards from the Credit page. All three treatments are live: drag to reorder, turn one off, turn it back on. What differs is where arranging happens, and therefore what the member is looking at while they decide.
Four rules hold in all three, and they are what makes this a layout feature rather than a settings page.
1. Hidden is not deleted, and the stored value is the hidden set plus the order, never the visible list. A widget shipped next month is absent from every stored layout, so a stored visible list would silently switch it off for every existing member. Stored as the negative, it appears for everybody and can then be turned off.
2. A hidden widget must never hide a fact. The "one of those limits is one you set" note under utilization and the point-value disclosure under rewards are the honest half of their figures. A summary widget that cannot carry its own caveat does not carry the figure either.
3. An empty widget does not hold its slot. Budgets with no budgets and transactions with no feed collapse out of the flow rather than sitting as titled boxes with nothing in them. The connect nudge is not a widget and cannot be arranged.
4. Every widget names where its full version lives, and both surfaces read the same helpers, so the summary and the page can be redundant but never contradict.
One consequence worth seeing before choosing. Today the page has a 1.5fr/1fr hero row (net worth beside plans) and two equal rows below it. Free ordering cannot keep that: the pairing is a property of the pair, and the member moves one card at a time. So all three treatments flow the widgets into equal halves, with a widget going full width when it lands on a row alone. Net worth loses its wider column. That is the cost of the feature, and it is the same in A, B and C.
D Chosen: a map made of the real cards, in a sheet
C's map with A's cards in it. Arrange opens a sheet over the page holding a scale model of the Overview, and every tile is the same markup the page renders, scaled with a transform and clipped to a uniform height, so a tile cannot show a card the Overview does not and a change to a card is a change to its tile by construction. Drag with a mouse or a finger, minus takes a widget off, Save writes the arrangement to the member and Cancel puts it back. The phone beside it is the same sheet at 375px: one column, because the Overview is one column there and a two-column map would describe an order the page does not have, with each tile clipped to the card's top strip at real size rather than shrunk, since a card at a third of 375px is not legible. Reload this page and the arrangement comes back, which is the persistence the real thing gets from a dashboard_layout column rather than from this preview's localStorage.

A Jiggle in place, on the real cards
Arrange turns the page itself into the editor. The cards keep their contents, gain a grip and a remove badge, and tilt just enough to say the page is in a mode. Widgets turned off drop into a shelf at the bottom that is only visible while arranging. This is the reference the issue names, and it is the only treatment where the member is looking at the actual card while deciding whether they want it. The cost is real: the drags are long (a full net-worth card is 300px tall, so moving it past three others is a scroll and a drop), and a jiggling page is the treatment most likely to need a keyboard fallback built beside it rather than after it.

B A panel beside the page, list on the right
Arrange opens a list of the widgets next to the page, and the page reorders live as the list is dragged. Eight short rows, each with a handle, the name, where its full version lives, and a switch. Nothing on the page moves under the cursor, so the drag targets are 40px rows rather than 300px cards, the whole set is visible at once without scrolling, and a switch is a control a keyboard and a screen reader already understand. The cost: the member is arranging names, one step removed from the thing itself, which is exactly the abstraction the iPhone reference avoids.

C The page shrinks to a map of itself
Arrange collapses the Overview into a scale model: the same grid, the same proportions, labels instead of contents. Eight tiles on one screen, so every drag is short and the whole arrangement is visible while making it, and dragging a tile is still dragging the widget rather than its name. The cost: the transition into the map is the one animation this feature genuinely needs (without it the page appears to have been replaced), and a tile is a promise about a card the member cannot see while they place it.
